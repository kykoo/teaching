GNSS-RTK

Global Navigation Satellite System

GNSS is a satellite-based system that provides Position, Velocity, and Precise time.

GNSS Operating Authority Nominal Satellites Notes
GPS USA (US Space Force) 24 (≈31 active) Most widely used; strong legacy support
Galileo EU (ESA/EUSPA) 24 (≈28 active) High civilian accuracy; excellent clocks
GLONASS Russia 24 (≈24 active) Better coverage at high latitudes
BeiDou China 30+ Hybrid constellation; strong Asia-Pacific performance

How does GNSS determine position?

GNSS measures signal travel time from satellites:

  1. Satellite sends a time-stamped signal
  2. Receiver measures the delay
  3. Distance = (speed of light) × (time delay)
  4. Receiver position found by trilateration

Three satellites enough?

Number of Satellites Required

  • 4 satellites
    • 3 for position (Easting, Northing, Height)
    • 1 to time-difference between Satellites and the GNSS receiver

Accuracy: what affects it?

Main factors that degrade accuracy:

  • Satellite geometry (DOP)
  • Atmosphere (ionosphere, troposphere)
  • Multipath reflections (buildings, bridges), Signal Blockage
  • Receiver + antenna quality

Typical accuracy (rough guide):

  • Smartphone: 1–5 m
  • Survey GNSS (RTK): a few cm

Limitations of vision-based system

  • Blocking line of sight.
  • Can also be caused by poor weather.
  • Camera stability (without fixed reference) is crucial.
  • Shadows on image in low sun.
  • Also deteriorating lighting conditions slows frame rate.
  • Ultimately DMS cannot work at night unless illuminated.
