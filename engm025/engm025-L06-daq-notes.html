Data Acquisition Concept

  • A transducer is a device that converts one form of energy into another, most commonly converting a physical quantity into an electrical signal (or vice versa).

  • A Sensor is a transducer which turns physical phenomena (such as acceleration, strain, or displacement) into electrical signals (voltage or current), or vice versa.

  • Computers are able to handle digitised data only.

  • Data Acquisition refers to the process of measuring physical or electrical phenomena by converting that electrical signal into a digital format that can be stored, processed, and analyzed, by a computer.

Analog to Digital Conversion

  • A analogue voltage is converted to \(n\)-bits digital world by an ADC.
  • An ADC maps a range of voltage values (commonly -5V to 5V, or -10V to 10V, or -3.3V to 3.3V) to \(n\)-bits binary numbers
  • For a 3-bits ADC, there are 8 different values represented by the 3-bits. The full range of voltage is mapped into one of 8 values.

Successive Approximation Converter

A successive approximation converter uses a comparator and counting logic to perform a conversion.

The first step in the conversion is to see if the input is greater than half the reference voltage. If it is, the most significant bit (MSB) of the output is set. This value is then subtracted from the input, and the result is checked for one quarter of the reference voltage.

This process continues until all the output bits have been set or reset. A successive approximation ADC takes as many clock cycles as there are output bits to perform a conversion.

Quantisation Error

For an ADC with \(n\)-bits

\[ \text{(Quantisation Error)} < \frac{\text{(voltage range)}}{2^{n+1}} \]

The above relation is explained as following.

  • Quantisation step (LSB)

For an \(n\)-bit ADC with full-scale input range \(V_{\text{FS}}\), the quantisation step (1 LSB) is

\[ \Delta = \frac{V_{\text{FS}}}{2^n}. \]

where \(\Delta\) is the quantisation step size (1 LSB).

  • Maximum quantisation error

With rounding to the nearest level, the quantisation error satisfies

\[ e_q \in \left[-\frac{\Delta}{2},\; +\frac{\Delta}{2}\right]. \]

Hence the maximum magnitude is

\[ |e_q|_{\max} = \frac{\Delta}{2} = \frac{V_{\text{FS}}}{2^{n+1}}. \]

Aliasing of Signals above Fs/2

Nyquist Frequency

For a signal sampled at a sampling frequency \(f_s\), the Nyquist frequency is defiend as

\[ f_N = \frac{f_s}{2} \]

  • \(f_N\) is the highest frequency that can be uniquely represented
  • Frequencies above \(f_N\) will alias into lower frequencies
  • Each sinusoid needs at least two samples per cycle

Aliasing example

  • Sampling frequency: \(f_s = 100\ \text{Hz}\)
  • Nyquist frequency: \(f_N = 50\ \text{Hz}\)

A signal at \(70\ \text{Hz}\) appears at:

\[ f_{\text{alias}} = |f_s - f| = |100 - 70| = 30\ \text{Hz} \]

Aliasing Example

  • Rotor speed ~400 ± 20 RPM
  • blade passes at 4x400/60 ~ 27 Hz
  • iPhone frame rate fs = 30 Hz
  • Alias appears at 27-30 = -3 Hz, i.e., rotating backwards

Anti-Aliasing Filter (AAF)

  • fs is sample rate (Hz), fs/2 is ‘Nyquist frequency’ fN.
  • Apply analog low pass filter before digital sampling of analog signal to ensure components above fN are not in the digitised signal.
  • In modern instruments AAF is applied automatically as shown below, so that signal attenuates (at least) 50% at fN.
  • Frequency information above 0.8 fN i.e. 0.4fs is ignored.

Two Approaches

  • Analogue vs Digital Low-pass Filters

  • Analogue Low-pass filter which is an analog electric circuits using capacitors C and resistors R, they may be simple passive RC networks or more effective active (powered) filters.

  • Digital Low-pass filter after an oversampling on ADC with a higher sampling frequency that ADC supports.

  • e.g. Acc has the max freq comp of 10 Hz, and we want to keep signal < 1 Hz, then ADC with 40Hz, and apply a low-pass filter (AAF) with fc = 1 Hz, followed by a decimation with a factor of 20.

Choosing An Appropriate DAQ

Key performance indices of DAQs

  • Input voltage range: ±10V, ±5V, ±3.3V etc
  • Number of ADC bits
  • Supported Sampling frequencies

CHOICE OF # of bits in your ADC

  • 24 bits ADCs become common these days and may be enough for most applications
  • 16 bits ADCs may be OK or not depending on your input voltage range.
  • Quantisation error below the sensor noise level doesn’t help

CHOICE OF Fs

  • At least 2 times higher than the maximum frequency component in the signal
  • If needed, an analogue AAF can be used, or a digital AAF is used if a high Fs is supported by ADC
